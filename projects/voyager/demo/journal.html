Mission Journal

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, from the launch pad to interstellar space

197720 August

Voyager 2 launches Voyager 2

A Titan IIIE–Centaur lifts the second-numbered probe off from Cape Canaveral first, on the slower path that keeps Uranus and Neptune in reach.

19775 September

Voyager 1 launches Voyager 1

Sixteen days later the first probe follows on a faster path; it overtakes its twin before the end of the year.

19795 March

Jupiter Voyager 1

The closest approach to Jupiter. The pictures of Io show volcanoes erupting on another world for the first time, and a faint ring round the planet.

19799 July

Jupiter again Voyager 2

The second flyby watches the Great Red Spot turn and maps the icy, cracked surface of Europa.

198012 November

Saturn and Titan Voyager 1

A close pass by Titan, whose thick haze hides its surface, bends Voyager 1 up and out of the plane of the planets for good.

198125 August

Saturn Voyager 2

The rings resolve into thousands of ringlets, with spokes and braids that the flyby films as they move.

198624 January

Uranus Voyager 2

The first visit to the tilted planet finds new moons and new rings, and a magnetic field pointing far from its axis.

198925 August

Neptune and Triton Voyager 2

The last planet of the Grand Tour: the Great Dark Spot, the fastest winds measured in the solar system, and geysers on Triton.

199014 February

The family portrait Voyager 1

Looking back, Voyager 1 takes sixty frames of the planets from beyond Neptune’s orbit; the Earth is less than a pixel, the pale blue dot.

199817 February

The farthest Voyager 1

Voyager 1 passes Pioneer 10 to become the most distant object people have made.

200416 December

The termination shock Voyager 1

The solar wind slows below the speed of sound: the probe is in the heliosheath, the Sun’s outer bubble.

200730 August

The shock, again Voyager 2

Voyager 2 crosses the termination shock several times as it moves in and out, a few billion kilometres from Voyager 1’s crossing.

201225 August

Interstellar space Voyager 1

The density of the plasma around the probe jumps: Voyager 1 has left the heliosphere, the first human-made object to do so.

20185 November

Interstellar space, again Voyager 2

Voyager 2 follows. Its plasma instrument, which still works, measures the crossing directly.

2024April

A memory chip routed round Voyager 1

After months of unreadable data, engineers move the flight data system’s code away from a failed chip; the science data comes back.

Todayand onwards

Still listening Both

The probes turn instruments off one by one to save power, and keep sending what they can over the Deep Space Network, more than twenty hours away at the speed of light.

The Golden Record on each probe carries greetings in 55 languages, sounds and music of the Earth, and 115 pictures.
